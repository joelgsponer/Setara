# Setara: a learning device for village schools

*Setara means "star" in Dari.*

In many village schools in Afghanistan, one teacher with little training
stands in front of up to fifty children of different ages. There is no
computer, little power and no internet.

Setara is our answer: a small learning app on a game handheld that costs
less than fifty francs. With a loudspeaker it becomes a help for the whole
class.

## What Setara does

**For the children.** English, reading Dari, math and health, in short
rounds of two to four minutes. Everything is spoken: a child who cannot read
yet can use it from the first moment. Wrong answers are never punished; they
simply come back later.

**For the teacher.** The device leads step by step through a complete
English lesson. The screen says in Dari what to do; one press plays the
correct English through the loudspeaker. The evening before, she can
practise the lesson by herself. She stays the teacher; the device gives her
a voice and a plan.

**Health and first aid.** Washing hands, safe water, what to do for burns,
cuts, poisoning or someone who has collapsed, and when a sick relative
belongs in the clinic.

**No internet, no data.** Everything is on a memory card. The device never
needs a connection and stores no names, no photos and nothing about
individual children.

## Where we are

Setara is a prototype. The software runs on a computer today. Next, we test
it on the first devices, and Dari-speaking teachers and a health
professional review all of the content. Only then does a device go into a
classroom.

Setara is open: the program and the content are free to reuse and to
translate.
