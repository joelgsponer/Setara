Generated by `content/math/generate.py`; do not edit the CSV files, change the

## What it teaches (spec, section 8, level 1)

Count, read and compare 0 to 20 in both digit systems; add and take away with
objects, never across ten; shapes, longer and shorter, more and less.
10 games, 71 rounds, 1240 items (version 1: 6 games, 44 rounds, 322 items).

## Pools (version 2)

Every game has `"draw": 8` (Twin Tiles: 6 pairs). A round is a pool of 16 to
24 questions and a play asks eight of them, a different eight each time. The
generator makes each pool with a fixed seed per round, spreads the answers
evenly over the round's number range and allows no answer to be right more
than three times in a pool. Where a round has only a handful of possible
answers at all (four shapes, twelve hours, the four signs) they are balanced
instead; in a sorter every basket gets at least half its even share. Pools
below 16 exist only where the material is limited; they are listed below.
`python3 content/math/generate.py --check` checks all of this again from the
finished rows and prints the table.

Smaller pools: Share the Bread 01 and 02 (12 things), 03 (15): the library
has only that many things of these shapes. Twin Tiles 01 (12): a pair card
shows at most twelve things to count.

## Decisions taken

- **Round 1 of the first games had to grow.** Sixteen questions with no
  answer more than three times need at least six different answers, so
  counting starts with 1 to 6 (was 1 to 5), adding with sums to 8 (was 5),
  the scales with 1 to 6 (was 1 to 3).
- **Level 1 still never crosses ten** (asserted): doubles are those of 1 to
  5 and of 10; teens are added and taken away without carrying.
- **The row with a gap** is shown as `1 2 _ 4` and spoken «یک، دو، جای خالی،
  چهار». A question card holds about eight digits at 24 px; a longer row is
  shortened to three numbers.
- **True or false** uses the library's tick and cross as baskets, spoken
  «درست» and «غلط».
- **Word problems** are one or two spoken sentences from village life
  (sheep, eggs, bread, afghani, steps, cups of water, walnuts, girls and boys
  in a class), with the picture of the things where one exists. The wrong
  choices are the typical mistakes: the other operation, one more, one less.
  The help says the way first («چیزها کم شد، پس منفی می‌کنیم») and then the sum.
  Each story has a largest number it still makes sense with.
- Questions that were the same sentence for every item now name the thing or
  read the numbers aloud («پنج، هشت، شش، دو. این عددها را از کوچک به بزرگ بچین»).

## For the human reviewer

A Dari-speaking teacher should decide (the points of `docs/review-notes.md`,
"Math 1 to 3", still stand):

1. «دو چند» for double; «جای خالی» for the gap in a row; «پیش بشمار» / «پس
   بشمار» for counting on and back.
2. «درست» / «غلط» for true and false (or «صحیح» / «غلط»).
3. «بین پنج و هفت»; «همین قدر» for "just as many"; «کمتر از ده / بیشتر از ده».
4. New nouns: «بره», «چوچه مرغ», «بادنجان رومی», «قدم», «کوزه», «دسترخوان»,
   «چراگاه», «لانه», «قیمت‌تر» (costs more).
5. The story sentences themselves: every template is in `STORIES` in the
   generator, about forty of them. No names are used; «خواهر» and «برادر»
   take turns having more.
6. A banknote (`note`) is sorted as a rectangle.
7. Is sixteen to twenty-four questions per pool the right size, and eight per
   play the right length for a six-year-old?

Nobody has listened to the audio, and nothing has been tried on a handheld or
with children.
