# Dari reading 6: Fluent reader (`fa-6`)

**Nobody who speaks Dari has read or heard this pack.** It was written by an
AI (Claude). Every text needs a Dari-speaking educator, and the information
texts need a teacher who checks the facts. The English of every text is in
`content/i18n/dari6.csv`; `syllabus.md` in this folder gives every unit with
its text, key words, reading skill and games, for the teacher guide.

## What the pack teaches

Curriculum, level 6: *read information texts, proverbs and poems; tell apart
letters that sound alike; fluent reading*. Four strands; a unit's games
carry its number as their round number.

| Game | Engine | Rounds | What the child does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Texts to Learn From (`a`) | story | 01-10 | ten information texts of 169 to 202 words: three key words, an opening question, four reading pages, a question after each |
| Proverbs (`b`) | story | 11-14 | twelve proverbs, three a round: the proverb, a page with its plain meaning and an everyday example, "what does it say?", then two little situations: which proverb fits? |
| Poems (`c`) | story | 15-19 | four new poems and one couplet of Ferdowsi: key words, the poem on one page, a prose page that explains it, four questions (topic, rhyme, image, meaning) |
| Letters That Sound Alike (`k`) | story | 20, 20b | the explanation: which letters sound alike, why, that each word is learned by eye, that one letter can change the meaning |
| Letter Baskets (`d`) | sorter | 20-25 | 20: sorts eleven letters into four sounds. 21-25: hears a word, sees it with a gap (؟) and puts it in the basket of the right letter |
| Which Is Right? (`e`) | listen_pick | 21-26 | hears a word in a sentence and picks the right spelling among spellings that sound the same (صابون، سابون، ثابون) |
| Text Builder (`f`) | builder | 01-14 | puts the steps of a text in order; builds sentences and answers from word tiles; builds each proverb |
| Fact Baskets (`g`) | sorter | 02 04 06 08 10 | in the text / not in the text; from the wheat text or the bread text; whose job (author, illustrator, printing house). **The cards are not read aloud** |
| Meaning Pairs (`h`) | pairs | 01 05 14 18 | word and picture; opposites; the two key words of one proverb; old or rare poem word and plain word |
| The Missing Word (`i`) | listen_pick | 03 07 09 13 16 | the last word of a sentence, riddles, opposites; the ending of each proverb; the rhyme of a word |
| What Do You Remember? (`j`) | listen_pick | 10, 20 | which text, which proverb, which poem: answers are pictures |

Every game except the story games is a pool (`"draw"`).

## How `say_fa` is used

Reading pages are spoken exactly as written, without the reading-purpose
lead-in of level 5: the text is on the screen at once and a fluent reader is
ahead of the voice (A cuts the voice and turns the page). The poem page is
spoken line by line with a comma between the lines. Fact Baskets cards have
no speech at all (the sorter allows that): there, and on the answer cards of
every picking game, reading is the exercise. Where an answer is longer than
a card can show at 30 px, the choices are written out under the question,
numbered ۱ ۲ ۳, and the cards carry only the numbers (38 questions); the
question is spoken, the choices are not. The position of the right choice is
mixed by the script (first 32, second 30, third 25 times).

## Fact sheet for the information texts

Written from general knowledge, not from a document. A teacher should check
each line; the texts say no more than this.

1. **Bread.** Wheat is ground between millstones into flour. Dough is flour,
   water, a little salt and leaven (old dough or yeast). Kneaded, covered,
   left warm for one to two hours; the leaven makes gas bubbles and the
   dough rises, which makes bread soft. Flat pieces are stuck to the hot
   inner wall of a clay oven (tandoor) and bake in a few minutes.
2. **Honey bee.** Thousands of bees in one hive; one queen, larger, lays
   the eggs; workers gather nectar with their tongues, store it in wax cells
   and fan it with their wings until enough water is gone: honey. Pollen
   sticks to the bee and is carried to other flowers; without it many
   fruit trees (apple, almond, apricot) set little fruit. A bee stings in
   defence.
3. **Water cycle.** The sun warms sea, lakes and rivers; water evaporates
   (wet clothes dry the same way); vapour cools high up into tiny drops:
   cloud; drops join and fall as rain, or snow when it is very cold; water
   runs off in streams and rivers or sinks into the ground (wells, springs).
   The amount of water on Earth stays about the same.
4. **Silk Road.** For more than two thousand years, from about the 2nd
   century BC, trade routes (many, not one) linked China with the lands to
   the west. Caravans of camels and horses; caravanserais for the night.
   Goods: silk, spices (pepper, cinnamon), glass, paper, precious stones;
   lapis lazuli from Badakhshan was traded far. Balkh, Bamiyan and Herat
   lay on the routes. Knowledge travelled too, paper-making among it.
5. **The eye.** Light reflected from things enters through the pupil; the
   iris makes the pupil smaller in bright light and larger in the dark; the
   lens focuses an image on the back of the eye (retina, not named); the
   optic nerve carries the signal to the brain. Lids, lashes and tears
   protect. Never look at the sun; do not rub eyes with dirty hands; read
   in good light; blurred sight may need glasses.
6. **Moon.** A ball of rock without light of its own; the half facing the
   sun is always lit; it goes round the Earth in about a month (phases:
   29.5 days); we see more or less of the lit half: new moon (between Earth
   and sun, not visible), crescent, half, full after about two weeks, then
   waning. Calendars have long been kept by it.
7. **A river.** Rises in mountains from snowmelt and springs; streams join;
   it flows downhill and over thousands of years cuts a valley, carrying
   sand and soil; slower and wider on flat land; people settle beside it and
   lead water to fields; highest in spring; floods. Ends in a larger river,
   a lake or the sea. The Helmand is the longest river of Afghanistan (about
   1,150 km), rises in the mountains west of Kabul and ends in the Hamun
   lakes in the south-west. The Kabul River flows east into the Indus.
8. **Wheat.** A grass. In many places sown in autumn after ploughing; rests
   under snow, which protects it from frost and waters it in spring; an ear
   holds roughly 30 to 50 grains; ripens yellow in early summer; cut with
   sickles and bound in sheaves; threshed and winnowed in the wind (straw
   blows away, grain falls); grain to the mill, straw is winter fodder.
9. **Snow leopard.** High mountains of Central Asia; in Afghanistan the
   Pamir and Wakhan in Badakhshan. Thick long fur, wide paws that act like
   snowshoes, a long thick tail for balance and to wrap round the face;
   whitish grey with dark spots, hard to see; solitary, active at dawn and
   dusk, shy of people ("ghost of the mountains" is a common nickname). Eats
   mountain goats (ibex) and other mountain animals. A few thousand remain
   (estimates 4,000 to 6,500). Villagers in Wakhan work as rangers.
10. **A book.** Author writes and rewrites; illustrator; an editor corrects;
    paper mostly from wood pulp, also recycled paper; presses print large
    sheets, thousands an hour; folded, cut, sewn or glued, cover put on.
    Before printing, books were copied by hand by calligraphers, took
    months, and were rare and expensive.

Simplifications: pollen is "the flower's yellow dust"; nectar is «شیره
گُل»; the retina is "the back of the eye"; evaporation is explained by
warmth only; the moon is "like a mirror".

## The poems

- **Units 15 to 18** («باران», «کتاب من», «شب قریه», «چهار فصل»)
  were written for this pack: three rhymed couplets each, in simple words.
  They are new and can be published under CC BY-SA. They are verse by an
  AI: the rhymes are meant to hold in Dari vowels (نم‌نم / کم‌کم،
  سیراب / آب، خندان / باران، یار / کار، پند / قند، دروازه / تازه، ستاره /
  کناره، روشن / چمن، آرام / بام، باران / فراوان، می‌ریزد / می‌خیزد،
  می‌بارد / دارد), but the metre is loose. A Dari poet or teacher should
  mend them or replace them with known children's verse that is free to
  use. «روشن / چمن» is the weakest rhyme.
- **Unit 19** is one couplet of Ferdowsi from the opening of the Shahnameh
  (about AD 1000, public domain), quoted as the writer is certain it
  stands: «توانا بود هر که دانا بود / ز دانش دل پیر برنا بود». It is
  written with vowel marks (بُوَد، بُرنا، دلِ) so that the voice does not
  read «بود» as "was". No other classical line is used.

## The letters that sound alike, and one point where the brief is not followed to the letter

Taught as spelling by eye: the groups ث س ص, ز ذ ض ظ, ت ط, ح ه are spoken
alike in Dari; the reason given is that the words came from Arabic, where
the sounds differ; there is no easy rule; each word is learned on its own;
mistakes are normal, also for adults. 83 words in five rounds and 15 more in
the mixed round, all common, each with a sentence that gives its meaning.

**ق and غ.** The task for this pack said to teach that ق and غ "sound alike
in Dari". As far as the writer knows that is true of Iranian Persian, but
not of Afghan Dari, where ق (a hard stop) and غ (a soft, drawn-out sound)
are usually told apart in careful speech. Teaching Afghan children that
they are one sound could be wrong. So the pack says: "the sounds of these
two letters are **very close**; ق is harder and shorter, غ softer and
longer; very often the ear hears them as one and we make mistakes in
writing; so learn these word by word too". Round 20 of Letter Baskets, which
sorts letters by sound, leaves ق and غ out for the same reason. A Dari
speaker must decide; if the two are plainly different for the children,
unit 25 is a listening exercise and its introduction should say so. **The
stand-in voice is Iranian and will pronounce them alike**, which makes this
unit misleading until the real voice is in.

Other decisions in this strand:

- **The wrong spellings are non-words** that sound exactly like the word
  (سابون). Words whose other spelling is itself a word were left out: صد /
  سد (used only in the explanation), نظر / نذر, ذره / زره, غلط / غلت, حال /
  هال, هوا / حوا, بهار / بحار, غار / قار, زن / ظن, and اطاق / اتاق, which
  has two accepted spellings. The script refuses a wrong spelling that
  equals another word of the lists. In English mode all spellings of a word
  carry the same gloss, so the gloss gives nothing away.
- **The gap** in Letter Baskets is a question mark inside the word (؟ابون،
  م؟ل). The letters beside it are drawn unjoined.
- **Letter names** as in levels 1 and 2 (سین، صاد، ثِ، زِ، ذال، ضاد، ظا،
  تِ، طا، قاف، غین), with «حِ جیمی» and «هِ دوچشمه» for the two h.
- Final ه that is only a vowel (خانه) is not used as an example of the
  sound h. ع and ا / ء, which also sound alike, are not taught: the
  curriculum does not list them.

## Other decisions

- **Homographs** carry vowel marks, also on the page: گُل، پُر، خُرد، دُم،
  گِرد، گَرد (dust), دَور، تَر، کِرم، کِشت، می‌بُرد، می‌بُرند، تُنُک،
  پُف. «ده» and «نه» are avoided («نخیر»).
- **River and sea** as in the rest of the app: دریا is a river, بحر the
  sea, جهیل a lake.
- **Pictures:** 7 new ones (`pictures.txt`): dough, tandoor, sickle,
  snow_leopard and three scenes. Everything else is from the library.
- The sheets were written out by a script; the CSVs are the source now.
