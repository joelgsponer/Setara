# English story book (`en-3-stories`): sources, decisions, open points

An optional add-on to English level 3: sixteen short stories to read, for
children of about 9 to 14 who can already read Latin letters and have done
English levels 1 and 2. It is a separate zip; leave it off the card and
nothing of it is there. **Nothing here has been read by a teacher, a native
speaker of Dari or a native speaker of English.** It was written by an AI
(Claude).

## What it is

Four games, all on the `story` engine, one story per round (16 rounds,
202 pages: 113 reading pages, 41 picture pages, 48 questions; about 4,800
words of English).

| Game | Rounds | Language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a First Stories | 4 | present tense, almost only words of levels 1 and 2 |
| b Animal Fables | 4 | present tense, more new words (fly, drop, push, net, race) |
| c Clever Tales | 5 | present tense with the level 3 material: days, time, clothes, home, numbers to 100 |
| d Stories from Yesterday | 3 | past tense, introduced with two Dari-and-English example pages per story |

Each story: a picture page with the title, 6 to 8 reading pages of 25 to 55
words (the text has the whole screen and is spoken and lit), one or two
picture pages in between, and three questions placed after the passage they
ask about. Every story has at least one "why" question. Each round starts
with a one-line Dari introduction that tells what the story is about; this
is also where the child hears the Dari for the main new words (crow, jug,
net, tortoise, turnip ...).

## Where the stories come from

Everything is free to publish under CC BY-SA 4.0. No modern published text
was copied or paraphrased; the traditional tales were retold from the plot
alone, in new words, at this reading level.

Names are invented, common first names (Mina, Hamid, Jawad, Farida, Shirin,
Omid, Soraya, Bashir, Nadia, Yusuf, Zarmina, Parwin). Nasruddin is the
folk-tale figure, not a person.

## Decisions

- **Girls and boys.** Human heroes: Mina, Shirin, Farida and the old woman,
  Parwin, Zarmina and her grandmother / Hamid, Jawad, Omid, Nasruddin
  (twice). The four children with the elephant are two girls and two boys.
  Among the animals the crow, the mouse (lion story), the tortoise, the
  young dove and the white goat are "she".
- **Nothing frightening.** The hunter in the two net stories only loses his
  net. The lion does not threaten to eat the mouse. The goats do not fall.
  The wolf story (The Boy Who Cried Wolf) was left out on purpose.
- **No teaching of religion, no politics.** "Mulla" is only the name the
  folk figure is known by.
- **Questions.** Choices are pictures or very short written English
  (`t:`), because a choice card holds one line: every written choice was
  measured to fit at 24 px. The right choice is listed first in 9 of 48
  questions (the app also mixes the cards). The question is shown in
  English and spoken in both languages; a wrong choice gets a gentle Dari
  sentence that gives the answer and asks the child to read again.
- **Order of speech.** The builder speaks `say_fa` before `say_en` when a
  row has both, so the questions are heard in Dari first, then English.
  The brief asked for English first; that cannot be chosen in the sheet.
- **Dari help for the past tense** (game d): two picture pages per story
  give a pair such as "Today she pulls. Yesterday she pulled." with the
  Dari said first («امروز کش می‌کند. دیروز کش کرد.»). Covered this way:
  pull/pulled, is/was, go/went, see/saw, say/said, come/came. Other
  irregular forms occur without help: had, put, "came off"; "fell",
  "took" and "stood" were written around.
- **New words.** Each story brings in roughly 8 to 12 words that levels 1
  to 3 do not teach (for example hungry, thirsty, jump, sweet, heavy,
  tired, thin, beak, idea, net, hunter, race, slow, fast, wings, rich,
  count again, dark, soup, fire, hay, turnip, pull, wind, narrow). They are
  repeated inside the story and most are carried by a picture or by the
  Dari introduction, but they are not drilled. Level 3 is expected to be
  finished or under way.
- **Pictures.** 12 new scenes (listed in `pictures.txt`); all other
  pictures are from the shared library. Scenes from the Dari read-along
  stories are reused for the cat, the young tree and the water-carrying
  boy, so those characters look like the children of those
  stories.

## For the human reviewer

English (a fluent speaker, ideally a teacher of beginners):

- Is the level right after English 2 and during English 3? The stories are
  harder than the level packs on purpose, but game a may still be too hard
  for a class that has only heard English.
- The present-tense telling of fables ("The fox comes to a garden") is the
  graded-reader convention; confirm it is wanted.
- Comparatives appear unexplained in three places: "stronger than you",
  "I am bigger", "it is better to walk".
- Contractions used: can't, don't, let's. "Miaow", "cluck", "Plop", "POP",
  "Whoo" are sound words the voice may read oddly.
- Speech has not been heard. Long pages are spoken as one clip; direct
  speech is all in the one (woman's) voice.

Dari (a Dari-speaking educator):

- Words chosen: زاغ (crow), نول (beak), کوزه (jug), جال (net), شکارچی
  (hunter), سنگ‌پشت (tortoise), خرگوش (hare), مسابقه (race), کبوتر (dove),
  شلغم (turnip), شوربا (soup), بالاپوش (coat, as in English 3), کالا
  (clothes), ظرف آب (water can; perhaps بشکه), مانده (tired), تیله کردن
  (to push), ایستاد شدن (to stop), خنک خوردن (to feel cold), دریا (river),
  برامد / نبرامد (came out / did not), بند ماندن (to be caught), پولدار
  (rich), پشیمان (sorry).
- «شیر» (lion) is written like milk and tap; the voice may say it wrong in
  the lion story's introduction and answers.
- «خُرد» and «نُه» carry a vowel mark for the voice, as in the other packs.
- "Mulla Nasruddin" is written «ملا نصرالدین». Is this the wanted form, and
  are the two anecdotes welcome locally? Both make gentle fun of him and of
  a rich host.
- The Dari past-tense examples are in the third and first person without a
  pronoun («دیروز رفتم»). Good enough as a hint?
- The introductions say where a tale comes from ("from Europe", "from
  Russia", "from the old book Kalila and Dimna"). Keep or drop?

Pictures (a local reviewer):

- Every new scene with people: the rider on the donkey, the two men at the
  dinner cloth, the old woman and the girl at the fire, the girl with the
  hen, the family pulling the turnip (a man pulls his wife, a girl pulls her
  grandfather), the girl in the wind, the girl and the boy with the elephant.
- `scene_doves_net` shows the doves carrying the net, not caught under it.
- `scene_turnip_pull`: the turnip is a purple mound with leaves; check that
  children see a turnip.
- The page "He can see nine donkeys" shows the library donkey nine times.
- Thing pictures stand in as story pages in places (fox, grapes, mouse,
  donkey, stone, coat, elephant, shop, sun, dog, goat).

Use in class:

- A round has 11 to 14 pages and takes longer than the usual two to four
  minutes: about five to eight minutes with speech.
- The app shows the question in English. A child who cannot read it still
  hears it in both languages, and the written choices are not read aloud
  (`read_choices` does not exist for stories).
