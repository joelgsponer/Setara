# Sources and review status of garden-4-rights (My rights, your rights)

## Read this first

- **This pack is optional.** It is a separate zip. Where it is left off the
  card, nothing of it is there. The local team decides class by class.
- **It was written by Claude**, the Dari and the English both. **It has not
  been reviewed by a Dari-speaking educator, and it has not been read by the
  local team.**
- **It must not be installed anywhere before the local team has read it and
  agreed.** Talk about rights, and about girls' schooling, can put a teacher
  or a class at risk where the programme works. Only the local team can judge
  that. If in doubt, leave the pack out.
- `content/i18n/rights.csv` carries the faithful English of every Dari text of
  the pack, so that someone who does not read Dari can judge what is said.
  The English is not a check of the Dari.
- The 21 pictures in `pictures.txt` are not drawn yet. A local reviewer
  approves every picture of a person.

## The basis

The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) in its child-friendly
form, with the idea of human dignity from the Universal Declaration of Human
Rights. Only these ten ideas are used, in a child's words:

| # | Idea | CRC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Every child matters the same: girl or boy, big or small, rich or poor, whatever language they speak at home, with or without a disability | art. 2 |
| 2 | Every child has a name and belongs to a family that cares for them | art. 7, 8, 9 |
| 3 | Every child has the right to food, clean water, a home and health care | art. 24, 27 |
| 4 | Every child has the right to learn | art. 28, 29 |
| 5 | Every child has the right to play and to rest | art. 31 |
| 6 | Every child has the right to be safe: nobody may hurt a child; no work that is dangerous or that stops them from learning | art. 19, 32 |
| 7 | Every child may say what they think and be listened to, and listens to others in turn | art. 12, 13 |
| 8 | A child with a disability has the same rights, and the right to help so that they can join in | art. 23 |
| 9 | Rights go together with respect: my rights end where I would hurt someone else's; I treat others as I want to be treated; we help the one who is left out | UDHR art. 1, 29 (dignity, duties to others) |
| 10 | If you or a friend are not safe or are being hurt: it is not your fault; tell an adult you trust; keep telling until someone helps | art. 19 |

Neither document is named or quoted anywhere in the pack. The children hear
only the ideas.

## Which game teaches what

| Game | Engine, rounds | Ideas |

| `rights_tell`, "Tell an adult you trust" (9 pages, 3 decisions) | story, 1 round | 10 |

| `rights_needs` round 03: fair / not fair | sorter, 8 things | 7, 9 |
| `rights_needs` round 04: learning / play and rest / health | sorter, 8 things | 3, 4, 5 |
| `rights_pick` round 01: which picture shows the right to ... | listen_pick, 8 | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 |
| `rights_pick` round 02: whose rights are these? | listen_pick, 8 | 1, 2, 8 |
| `rights_pick` round 03: what do we do? | listen_pick, 8 | 6, 7, 9, 10 |
| `rights_talk`, the class session for the teacher (12 pages, 3 questions) | story, 1 round | 1 to 10, one short statement a page |

| `rights_pairs` round 02: a need and who or what helps | pairs, 6 | 2, 3, 4, 5 |

`rights_talk` is meant to be run by the teacher with the whole class through
Story Time: everybody listens to a page, and the class answers the three
questions together.

## Hard limits the pack keeps

- Nothing about government, the laws of a country, authorities, religion, war,
  politics, marriage, clothing rules, or who is to blame for anything. No
  sentence says that anybody's rights are being violated. No group is named.
- Parents and elders are shown with respect, as the people who care for and
  protect the child. An adult may make a kind, wise choice (the father who
  listens and decides that the donkey carries the water; the grandmother who
  listens). No adult is a villain. Conflicts are small and between children
  (leaving out, laughing at someone, taking a pen), or a child's own worry.
- "Every child, girl and boy, has the right to learn" is said as a plain fact
  in the same breath as the other rights. It is not the theme of the pack and
  it is not argued. It occurs twice, as the same sentence: on one page of the
  class talk, and as the explanation on Y of one listening question
  (`rights_pick` 02). Everywhere else the pack says "every child has the
  right to learn".
- Telling a trusted adult (idea 10) is gentle. Nothing is described beyond
  "someone hurts you or frightens you"; the story shows an older child who
  takes a pen and bread and says "do not tell anyone". No violence is shown
  or spoken.
- Girls and boys in equal measure and in the same roles. Girls: Shukria,
  Maryam, Fereshta, Zainab. Boys: Yusuf, Idris, Jawid. The story of the new
  pupil is set in a girls' class, the story of telling among boys; in the
  Yusuf story a big sister helps her brother. None of the names is used in
  garden-2 or garden-3. No real person is meant.

## Sentences a cautious local reviewer might question

The English is given; the Dari is in the sheets and in `i18n/rights.csv`.

1. "Every child, girl and boy, has the right to learn." (`rights_talk`
   page 5; `rights_pick` 02, on Y.) The one
   sentence about girls' learning. If it is too much for a place, the pack
   should not be installed there; the sentence "Who has the right to learn?
   Only one child, or all children?" (`rights_pick` 02) with the single girl
   and the single boy as the wrong pictures belongs to it.
2. "At home they speak another language." and "whatever language they speak at
   home" (`rights_story` 01; `rights_pick` 02: "A child speaks another
   language at home. Does that child have the same rights too?"). No language
   or group is named, but a listener may hear one.
3. "Every child matters the same: girl or boy, small or big, ..., with or
   without a disability." (`rights_story` 01, said by the teacher;
   `rights_talk` page 1; `rights_pick` 02.) "Matters the same" is said as
   "has equal worth" (ارزش برابر دارد). It is said of children only.
4. "Rich or poor, every child matters the same." and "A child's family has
   little money. Does that child have the same rights too?" (`rights_pick` 02.)
5. "Nobody may hurt a child." (`rights_talk` page 6; `rights_pick` 01.) Some
   may hear this as a judgement on how parents or teachers correct children.
   The pack says nothing about that, but the sentence is general.
6. "Children must not do work that is dangerous, or work that does not let
   them learn." and "Heavy water cans are not for a child." (`rights_story`
   02.) Many children in the villages carry water and mind animals. The story
   says that helping at home is good and lets the father decide; a family may
   still feel judged. "Chopping wood with the axe" is given as dangerous work.
7. The whole Yusuf story: two children tell their father that the work is too
   much, and the father changes it ("it is good that you told me"; "Father
   asks: what do you think?"). Children speaking up to a father, however
   respectfully, may not be acceptable everywhere.
8. "Every child may say what they think, and others should listen to them.
   And they listen to others too." (`rights_talk` page 9; `rights_pick` 01,
   03.)
9. "That boy had said: Do not tell anyone." and "Something that frightens you
   is not a secret to keep. Tell an adult you trust." (`rights_tell`.) It
   tells a child not to keep a secret that an older child asked for.
10. "If the first adult is not there, or could not help, tell another adult.
    Keep telling until someone helps." (`rights_tell`, `rights_pick` 03.) It
    admits that an adult may not be able to help. In the story the mother is
    only out at the neighbour's.
11. "the adults talk calmly with that boy's family" (`rights_tell`). The older
    boy is not punished or named; a reviewer may want this ending changed.
12. "Tell an adult you trust: mother, father, grandmother, teacher or health
    worker." Whether a teacher or a health worker should be named as someone
    a child tells is for the local team to decide.
13. "My right ends where your right would be harmed." (`rights_talk` last
    page.) Abstract; may be hard for the youngest and odd in Dari.
14. "A child who has a disability has the same rights. They have the right to
    be helped so that they can be together with the others." The word used is
    معلولیت; the crutch is چوب زیربغل. A local educator should confirm both
    words are the kind and usual ones.
15. "It is not your fault" is said as تقصیر تو نیست, not گناه تو نیست, to keep
    away from a word that also means sin. The educator should say which is
    natural for children.
16. The word "right" itself (حق) and the pack title "My rights, your rights"
    (حق من، حق تو). If the word is the problem, the pack cannot be repaired by
    editing and should be left out.
17. Mixed pictures: `children_all`, `play`, `class` (the game icon) and
    `fa_shout_help` show girls and boys together. Where that is not wanted,
    girls-only and boys-only versions are needed.
18. In the "need / nice to have" rounds, "another new pair of shoes, when you
    already have shoes", "a new cap", "a new coloured bag", "a radio" are put
    under nice to have. Shoes and warm clothes as such are needs; the wording
    tries to say so, and may still be read wrongly by a child without shoes.

## Other things to know

- The wrong choices of the stories only restate the rule; nobody is scolded.
- Reused pictures show other children than the new scenes do: the Yusuf story
  ends with `scene_boy_friends` and `scene_boy_sleeping` (the boy of
  garden-1), and the telling story uses `scene_omar_ali` and
  `scene_boys_goodbye` (the boys of english-1). The briefs in `pictures.txt`
  ask for the new scenes to match them.
- `tummy` (a boy with his hands on his tummy) stands for "a sick child" and
  `bowl_empty` for "a hungry child" in the pairs.
- The class talk leaves the words "whatever language" and "disability" out of
  its first sentence to keep it short; disability has its own page.
