# Sources and review status of garden-3 (Health 3: First aid)

**Not yet checked by a health professional.** Every medical statement in this
pack is taken from the project's first-aid fact sheet, pasted in full below.
The fact sheet is modelled on standard first-aid guidance (IFRC, WHO). Nothing
medical was added by the author of the sheets. Before this pack ships, someone
qualified must read the fact sheet and every text of the pack
(`content/i18n/firstaid.csv` carries the faithful English of every Dari text),
and a Dari-speaking educator must read and hear the Dari.

No doses, no names of medicines, no home remedies. The pictures listed in
`pictures.txt` are not drawn yet.

## Where the pack simplifies, or goes a little beyond, the fact sheet

Reasons and wording
- The fact sheet says what not to do but not why. After a wrong choice the
  stories give only a minimal reason: pastes on a burn "do not help and do
  harm"; earth and ash "are not clean"; with danger "if you get hurt too, two
  people need help". Elsewhere the wrong-answer speech only restates the rule.
- Butter, egg, mud, flour (burns) and tobacco, cobweb, cow dung (wounds) are
  spoken but have no picture. The pictures show toothpaste, oil, ice, earth, ash.

Burns
- The story takes the bracelet off while the burn is being cooled; the fact
  sheet does not say whether before, during or after. The step is left out of
  the ordering game.
- "Give sips of water" is in the story and the sorter, not in the ordering game.
- "Over a joint" is said as "on a joint" (بند). "Oozes" is said as "water comes from it".
- In the "home / clinic" round a "small burn on the arm, without a blister" is
  put under home. The exceptions (baby, old person, electricity, chemical,
  deep/white/black) are not repeated on that card.

Cuts, bleeding, bites
- "Raise the arm or leg if possible" is shown as holding the hand up.
- In the cut story the small cut from a rusty nail is first treated like a
  small cut (washed with soap and clean water, covered) and then taken to the
  clinic. This combines two points of the fact sheet.
- Tetanus: the father "asks the health worker about the tetanus vaccine".
  Nothing is said about what the clinic gives.
- Dog bite: the story says "the health worker starts the vaccine". The fact
  sheet only says that the vaccine works only if given in time. Rabies is
  named (مرض سگ دیوانه) and said plainly to kill.
- One listening question asks which animal's bite goes to the clinic the same
  day, with harmless animals (butterfly, sparrow, chick) as wrong choices. The
  rule "every animal bite goes to the clinic" is said in the cut story.
- Snake bite: only "go to the clinic now". The fact sheet gives no first aid.

Nosebleed
- Between ten and twenty minutes: "pinch for another ten minutes" was added
  in the first-aid review (see below).

Choking
- Abdominal thrusts are only named as "an adult pushes the belly firmly, up to
  five times". The technique is deliberately not described. In the story the
  father does it; back blows are given by an older girl.
- Babies: only "bring an adult quickly", as the fact sheet asks.

Someone has collapsed (life_support, first_steps round 04)
- Marked in the game introduction, on the first story page and in the round
  introduction as being for older pupils and teachers; a small child's job is
  to run for an adult.
- "Swap with another person when you are tired" comes from the task brief for
  this pack, not from the fact sheet. The fact sheet says "do not stop until
  help takes over or the person breathes".
- "Call for the clinic or a vehicle" is said as "tell them to bring a car and
  send word to the clinic".
- Step 5 (breathing: recovery position) is told as "if he were breathing";
  the story then follows step 6. The story does not say how it ends for the man.
- Only an adult casualty is shown. The fact sheet does not say whether
  anything differs for a child.
- Fainting appears only in danger_signs round 03.

When a relative is ill
- All eleven points of the "go to the clinic NOW" list are spoken in
  help_story. Only some have a picture and a question of their own.
- "Confused" is shown as "she does not recognise Parwin".
- "Cool if hot with fever" is now said as "take the blanket and extra clothes
  off; no icy water or ice" (first-aid review, see below).
- "Stool" is said as مواد غایطه, "urine" as ادرار. A reviewer may prefer the
  local everyday words.
- The "home" basket of do_dont round 05 is not in the fact sheet, which only
  says when to go to the clinic. Three cases were derived as the opposite of
  the clinic rules: a small cut from a clean thing that stopped bleeding, a
  small burn on the arm without a blister, a nosebleed that stopped after ten
  minutes. The basket says "we help at home and tell an adult". **A health
  professional should confirm this round in particular.**

## Changes made in the first-aid review

A review against standard lay first-aid guidance (IFRC, ERC/ILCOR, WHO) changed
the texts below. The reviewer is not a health professional and not a native
Dari speaker: **every added Dari sentence must be read by a Dari-speaking
educator and the content confirmed by a health professional.** These points go
beyond the fact sheet printed below, which was left as the author received it.

Burns
- burn_story, first_steps 01: cool only the burned place and keep the rest of
  the body warm (a small child gets cold quickly in twenty minutes of water).
- burn_story: do not touch someone who is still touching an electric wire;
  call an adult.
- do_dont 05: the "clinic" cards now say "after pouring cool water" (burn) and
  "we have washed the wound" (dog bite), so "clinic now" does not replace the
  first aid.

Bleeding and wounds
- cut_story, first_steps 02, danger_signs 02: when bleeding has not stopped
  after ten minutes, keep pressing on the way to the clinic. Very heavy
  bleeding: do not wait, press and call for help at once.
- cut_story, danger_signs 02: something stuck in a wound: do not pull it and
  do not press on it; press a clean cloth on both sides of it.
- cut_story: the helper washes his own hands with soap afterwards.
- first_steps 01 and 02: "and quickly tell an adult" added to the closing line.

Bites
- danger_signs 02: the bite or scratch of a cat, fox, jackal or any wild
  animal is treated like a dog bite (the question had only harmless animals
  as wrong choices).
- danger_signs 02: snake bite: keep the person calm and still; do not cut,
  suck or tie tightly above the wound (WHO). The fact sheet has no snake-bite
  first aid.

Nosebleed
- first_steps 03: still bleeding after ten minutes: pinch for another ten;
  after twenty, clinic.

Choking
- do_dont 04: the back-blows card said only "they cannot breathe"; it now says
  "food stuck in the throat and they cannot cough".
- first_steps 05: back blows only when the person cannot cough, leaning forward.
- life_support 02 (new page): after belly pushes the person is shown to the
  clinic; if the person becomes unconscious, lay them down, call for help
  again and start chest pushes.

Someone has collapsed
- life_support 01: "not breathing normally" now includes someone who only
  takes an occasional hard, noisy breath (gasping).
- life_support 01 (new page): a helper who is alone and unheard first runs for
  an adult.
- life_support 01: "someone who helps does a good thing, even if the ill
  person does not get better", so that a child does not carry the blame.

Fever
- help_story, danger_signs 03: "keep cool" is now "take the blanket and extra
  clothes off; no icy water or ice".

Home or clinic
- do_dont 05: the "home" basket now adds "if it gets worse, we go to the
  clinic".

Still open for a health professional (not changed)
- "Any burn with a blister goes to the clinic" is stricter than standard
  guidance (which sends blistered burns to care when larger than the person's
  hand, or on face, hands, feet, joints or genitals, or in a small child).
  Kept because it is the safer rule; decide whether it is workable locally.
- Chest compressions on a child casualty, and how long to continue when no
  clinic or vehicle can come, are not addressed in the pack.
- Burns: genitals, burns all the way round a limb, smoke or flame to the face,
  and clothes on fire are not mentioned.
- Diarrhoea: oral rehydration is not mentioned (only "sips of clean water").
- Whether the local clinic actually holds tetanus and rabies vaccine.

## The fact sheet

# The facts for the first-aid pack (use these; add nothing medical of your own)

Audience: village children about 8-14 and their teachers, far from a clinic. The first rule for a child is always: stay safe yourself, tell an adult, send or run for help. Keep every instruction short and doable with what a village home has: clean water, soap, clean cloth.

Burns and scalds
- Move away from the heat. Cool the burn with cool clean water (poured or running) for 20 minutes; at least 10.
- Take off rings, bracelets or tight clothing near the burn, unless stuck to the skin (then leave it).
- Do NOT put on ice, butter, oil, toothpaste, egg, mud, flour or any paste. Do NOT break blisters.
- Then cover loosely with a clean cloth. Give sips of water.
- Go to the clinic when: the burn is bigger than the person's own palm; on the face, hands, feet or over a joint; deep, white or blackened; has blisters; from electricity or a chemical; the person is a baby or an old person; or later it smells, oozes or the person gets fever.

Cuts and bleeding
- Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth and keep pressing for 10 minutes without lifting to look. Raise the arm or leg if possible.
- If blood soaks through, put another cloth on top; do not take the first one off.
- A small cut: when the bleeding has stopped, wash with clean water and soap, cover with a clean cloth, keep it clean and dry.
- Do NOT pull out something stuck deep in a wound. Do NOT put earth, ash, tobacco, cobweb or cow dung on a wound.
- Go to the clinic when: the bleeding does not stop after 10 minutes of pressing; the cut is deep or gaping; it was made by a dirty or rusty thing; something is stuck in it; it is a bite; or days later it is red, hot, swollen, has pus, or there is fever. Ask the clinic about the tetanus vaccine.

Animal bites (dog)
- Wash the bite at once with soap and plenty of running water for 15 minutes. Go to the clinic the same day, even if the wound is small (rabies is deadly and the vaccine works only if given in time).

Nosebleed
- Sit, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes, breathe through the mouth. Do NOT tilt the head back. Clinic if it does not stop after 20 minutes or it follows a blow to the head.

Choking (someone cannot cough, speak or breathe)
- If the person can cough: encourage them to keep coughing.
- If they cannot: shout for help. An older child or adult gives up to 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of the hand, the person leaning forward. If that fails, an adult gives up to 5 abdominal thrusts. Repeat. (Babies need a different technique: an adult does it. Do not teach it.)

Someone has collapsed and does not answer (basic life support, taught for teachers and older children; a small child's job is to fetch an adult fast)
1. Is it safe? (fire, road, electricity, water) Do not become a second victim.
2. Tap the shoulders and call loudly.
3. Shout for help; send someone to bring an adult and to call for the clinic or a vehicle.
4. Tilt the head back gently and lift the chin. Look, listen and feel: is the person breathing normally? (up to 10 seconds)
5. Breathing: turn the person onto their side (recovery position), head tilted back so the mouth points down a little; stay with them; keep checking the breathing.
6. Not breathing normally: start chest compressions. Kneel beside the person, heel of one hand on the centre of the chest, other hand on top, arms straight; push hard and fast, about two pushes every second, letting the chest come back up each time. Do not stop until help takes over or the person breathes. (Someone who has learned rescue breaths gives 2 breaths after every 30 pushes; if you have not learned it, only push.)
- Fainting: lay the person down and raise the legs; they should wake within a minute; if not, treat as collapsed.
- A fit (convulsion): do not hold the person down, put nothing in the mouth, move hard things away, turn them on their side when the shaking stops, go to the clinic.

A fall, a possible broken bone
- Do not move a person who may have hurt the neck or back unless there is danger. Keep an injured arm or leg still and supported. Go to the clinic.

When a relative is ill: go to the clinic NOW if the person
- cannot drink or breastfeed, or vomits everything;
- is very sleepy, hard to wake, or confused;
- has a fit;
- breathes fast or with difficulty, or the lips look blue;
- has a stiff neck with fever, or a baby under three months has any fever;
- has fever for more than two or three days;
- has blood in the stool or vomit, or diarrhoea with sunken eyes and no urine;
- has strong pain in the chest or belly;
- suddenly has a drooping face, a weak arm or leg, or cannot speak properly;
- is a pregnant woman with bleeding, a severe headache, a fit, or swollen face and hands;
- has been bitten by a snake or a dog, badly burned, or is bleeding heavily.
While waiting or on the way: keep the person warm (or cool if hot with fever), give small sips of clean water if they can swallow, never give a medicine unless a health worker has said so, and do not leave them alone.

Never write: doses, names of medicines, home remedies, anything religious, anything that blames the family.
